Buying property in Spain as a foreigner

The process is the same regardless of nationality, though non-EU buyers should budget more time for paperwork:

  1. Get your NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) — a tax ID number required for any property purchase, mortgage or significant financial transaction in Spain. Apply at a Spanish consulate before travelling, or in Spain at a police station (Oficina de Extranjería), or through a lawyer with power of attorney.
  2. Open a Spanish bank account — needed to pay the deposit, notary fees and set up direct debits for utilities and local property tax (IBI).
  3. Sign a reservation or deposit contract (contrato de arras) — typically 10% of the price, which is forfeited if the buyer withdraws without cause.
  4. Independent legal due diligence — a Spanish property lawyer checks the title (Registro de la Propiedad), outstanding debts, planning status and community fees before you commit.
  5. Sign before a notary (escritura pública) and pay the balance — this is when legal ownership transfers.
  6. Register the property at the Land Registry (Registro de la Propiedad) in your name.

Taxes for non-resident buyers

Purchase tax

Resale properties in Andalucía pay ITP (Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales) at 7% of the purchase price. New-build properties pay 10% VAT (IVA) plus 1-1.5% stamp duty (AJD) instead of ITP. Rates vary by Spanish region and can change — always confirm the current rate before completing a purchase.

Ongoing taxes as a non-resident owner

Non-resident property owners must file IRNR (Impuesto sobre la Renta de No Residentes) annually, even if the property isn't rented out:

  • Not rented: tax on "imputed income" — 1.1% to 2% of the property's cadastral value (depending on when it was last revised), taxed at 19% (EU/EEA residents) or 24% (non-EU residents).
  • Rented out: EU/EEA residents pay 19% on net rental income (expenses are deductible); non-EU residents pay 24% on gross rental income (no deductions allowed).

Source: Agencia Tributaria (Spanish Tax Agency), IRNR regulations in force in 2026. This is general information, not tax advice — non-resident tax treatment depends on your country of residence and any applicable double-taxation treaty with Spain.
